# Why I Want to Start Reading Fiction

![A quiet library filled with books](https://media.giphy.com/media/v1.Y2lkPTc5MGI3NjExNTQ0aHdlZzkyNnV6Z2txNnk1M3lvNmxkd2c1MmZ5MDVndjB1MnkxMSZlcD12MV9naWZzX3JlbGF0ZWQmY3Q9Zw/3o6fIT3Qst20RB4Xao/giphy.gif)

For a long time, I've mostly read nonfiction books. Self-help, philosophy, and spirituality have been my favourites.

I've read people like Buddha, Adi Shankaracharya, Osho, Socrates, and René Descartes. I've also read books that made me question my life, my thoughts, and even reality itself.

I love these books. Some of them have genuinely changed how I see the world.

But recently, I've started thinking about something.

**Am I looking at life from too few perspectives?**

## The world of ideas

Philosophy teaches me to question things instead of blindly believing them. Spirituality makes me look at my mind, my desires, and my ego. Self-help books give me ideas about how to live better.

I've learned to think about death, the meaning of life, and how little time we actually have.

I've learned not to waste my life worrying about what people think of me. Not to judge people too quickly. To observe my thoughts instead of believing everything that comes into my head.

These are beautiful ideas, and I still want to explore them.

But sometimes, I feel like I'm spending too much time thinking about life instead of actually experiencing it.

I can sit alone for hours, thinking about reality, truth, and the meaning of everything.

And at some point, I have to ask myself: *What about the life happening right in front of me?*

What about people, their stories, their feelings, their mistakes, and all the little things that make life interesting?

Maybe there is more to understand than just the big questions.

## What about understanding other people?

![A person reading a book in a quiet moment](https://media0.giphy.com/media/v1.Y2lkPTc5MGI3NjExdXhuZWdic3Vxc2Y4MzN2dmU5NG0ycjc4M21mand6dTl0czBpZWs4dCZlcD12MV9pbnRlcm5hbF9naWZfYnlfaWQmY3Q9Zw/Kpg5HBYyRqAE/giphy.gif)

One thing I genuinely enjoy is trying to understand people.

When I meet someone, I sometimes wonder what is going on inside their head.

Why do they think this way? Why do they behave like that? What experiences made them who they are? What do they fear? What makes them happy?

Two people can live in the same world and experience it in completely different ways.

Something that seems obvious to me might make no sense to someone else. Something I consider important might mean nothing to another person.

And that's interesting.

Instead of immediately judging someone, I want to understand how the world looks from their eyes.

But here's the thing: I can only meet so many people in my life. I'll never know what it's like to live every kind of life, grow up in every kind of family, or face every kind of problem.

**So how can I explore all those different worlds?**

Maybe through stories.

## Why fiction?

Honestly, I used to wonder why people spent so much time reading fictional stories.

If a book is made up, why should I spend hours reading it? Wouldn't that time be better spent learning something real?

But the more I think about it, the less sense that question makes.

A fictional story might not have happened in real life, but the emotions, struggles, fears, and choices inside it can be very real.

An author can create a character who thinks completely differently from me. Someone who makes choices I would never make. Someone whose life I would never understand by simply looking at them.

And as I follow that person's story, I get to see the world through their eyes.

I can experience a life I have never lived, explore a situation I have never faced, and understand a person I might never meet.

Of course, fiction won't automatically make me wiser or more understanding. A story can be shallow, just like a nonfiction book can be shallow.

But a great story can make me question my own assumptions without directly telling me what to believe.

And that sounds worth exploring.

## Maybe I don't need another answer

I've spent a lot of time looking for answers.

What is the truth? What is reality? What is the self? How should we live? What really matters when we're all going to die someday?

I still love these questions.

But maybe I don't need to turn everything into another question to solve.

Maybe I can read a story simply because it's interesting. I can care about a character, wonder what happens next, laugh at something stupid, or feel sad about something that never happened.

I don't need every book to teach me a lesson.

Sometimes, experiencing a story might be enough.

And perhaps that's another way of experiencing life: not always trying to explain everything, but allowing myself to feel, imagine, and explore.

## My first two books

So, I'm starting my fiction journey with two books.

![The first two books in my fiction journey](https://chatgpt.com/backend-api/estuary/public_content/enc/eyJpZCI6Im1fNmFlMzA1OWQ3MmM4ODE5MTliMmY5YjAwMmQwOWYxM2I6ZmlsZV8wMDAwMDAwMDY1MzQ4MjExYTYwNjQ0MzliNmU0MDIxZiIsImdpem1vX2lkIjpudWxsLCJ3aWQiOm51bGwsIm9pZCI6bnVsbCwic2lkIjpudWxsLCJjcyI6bnVsbCwiZm4iOm51bGwsImNkIjpudWxsLCJ0cyI6IjIwNzU1IiwicCI6InB5aSIsImNpZCI6IjEiLCJzaWciOiIxZmRhMDFjMWNlZjhjMzJhOGZmNWI4NDA5N2Q4ZmJjMjVjMDNjOGUzZDMyY2JjOGVmODUzNGMxNjFlYThlMTZmIiwidiI6IjAiLCJjZG4iOm51bGwsImNwIjpudWxsLCJtYSI6bnVsbH0=)

**1. Dark Matter — Blake Crouch**

A science-fiction thriller about reality, choices, and the different lives we might have lived. I'm excited to see where the story takes me and what questions it leaves me with.

**2. The Little Prince — Antoine de Saint-Exupéry**

A short, unusual story that explores life, love, friendship, and how we see the world. I've heard a lot about it, and I'm curious to experience it for myself.

I'm starting with these two because they offer different kinds of experiences. One promises a gripping story, while the other invites a quieter kind of reflection.

I don't know how I'll feel about either book yet. And that's part of the fun.

## Let's see what happens

I don't want to limit myself to one kind of book anymore.

I want to explore science fiction, fantasy, mysteries, literary fiction, and stories from different cultures. I want to encounter characters I love, characters I dislike, and characters I can't quite understand.

I might discover that some stories are a waste of my time. I might find books that completely change how I see something. I might simply enjoy a good story without taking anything deeper from it.

I'm fine with all of that.

I don't want to read only to collect knowledge or improve myself. I want to read because the world is interesting, people are complicated, and there are so many ways to experience life.

**Maybe nonfiction helps me understand ideas, while fiction lets me explore lives. I want both.**

This is the beginning of my fiction journey.

Let's see what happens.
